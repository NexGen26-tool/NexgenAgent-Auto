# Editing principles: how the owner thinks, not what the owner clicked

The owner re-edited desk V7, V8 and V9 by hand (Nov 19). Copying those edits would give every video the same
punchline shot, the same boxed CTA and the same text positions, which is the opposite of what the owner wants. These
are the reasons under the edits. Apply the reasons; choose the means per video, and try a better means when one fits.

## 1. One viewer, one watch, a phone
Every decision is judged as a stranger scrolling past, seeing the video once at phone size.
- That viewer notices: a different person, a product that changes shape, something moving by itself, a drawer on
  the wrong side of the person, a ghosted crossfade, slow motion, text over a face.
- That viewer does not notice: a slightly different shirt shade, a thin sheen on a top in a close-up, a room
  that is the same kind of room, the pull handles in a two-second close-up.
So fundamentals are strict and details are loose. When a reviewer fails a shot, ask "would the stranger see it?"
before cutting it, and ask "does this shot carry the sentence?" before keeping a clean but empty one.

## 2. The picture says the sentence
The owner chose shots by the verb of the line: the laptop closing for "the laptop closes", a hand pulling a drawer
for "drawers keep the clutter", the mug for the morning line. A clean shot that shows nothing the voice says is
weaker than a slightly imperfect shot that acts it out.
- Pick by meaning first (who does what to the product in this sentence), then check it against the fundamentals.
- Beyond the owner: when no take acts out a sentence, change the sentence's job on screen (a close-up that proves
  the claim, a before and after cut) rather than covering it with a pretty unrelated shot.

## 3. The product is the hero at the two moments that sell
The punchline and the end card show the product at its best: the best room, the best light, no person in the way.
The person sells the feeling in the middle; the product closes the argument.
- Beyond the owner: "at its best" is not one fixed shot. A reveal can be a hard cut from the problem to the piece, a
  push-in that lands on the defining part (the U leg, the live edge), a match cut from the person's hand to the
  empty piece. Vary it per video so a group of three does not end the same way twice; when the same hero shot must
  repeat, change its crop or zoom so the repeat reads as a choice.

## 4. Energy over coverage
No slow motion on anything that moves (the owner covered every 0.6-0.7x shot with a person or a hand). Shorter is
fine: he cut the CTA hold and the trailing silence. More shots at normal speed beat fewer stretched ones.
- Slowing is acceptable only on a still frame (an empty room, a static close-up), and only a little.
- Beyond the owner: when footage runs short, shorten the voice-over's slack or let a line of text carry a beat over
  a still product shot before reaching for slow motion.

## 5. Text carries the message, not labels
The owner rewrote lines into sentences that say something ("This solid wood office desk became the steady part of
my day"), named the product, echoed the voice word for word in the hook, and deleted lines that only restated the
picture ("Clutter / Out of sight"). Fewer lines, each one a thought.
- One accent word per line, on the word that carries the feeling ("steady", "clear").
- Beyond the owner: the line can do what the picture cannot (a number, a name, a contrast) instead of repeating
  what is on screen; a punchline can name the product in the brand's own words rather than the page title.

## 6. Text serves the picture
Placement follows the frame: low when the subject is up, staggered left and right for rhythm, never on a face or the
product's defining part. The CTA must stand apart from the footage (the owner used a dark box).
- Beyond the owner: the means of standing apart can change with the style (a box, a colour band, a clean area of
  the frame, a final still), as long as the CTA is the clearest thing on the end card.

## 7. Use what exists before asking for more
Reuse other takes of the same SKU (close-ups, empty product shots, before-states) before a remake; solve a model
limit with what the tool has (a camera-reference frame, the proof line) before asking for a feature. A remake is a
spend, and one remake is tested on one video first.

## 8. Quality is ours, not the owner's
The owner should never find a fundamental. Every save passes the gate; every hand edit of the owner is read, its
reasons written here, and the build changed so the next week needs less of it.

## How to use this file
At the edit plan (CLAUDE.md step 7) and the shot check (step 6b), read each video's sentences, apply 1-6, and write
in `log.md` one line per video on any choice that goes beyond the owner's own pattern and why, so the owner can
judge it.
