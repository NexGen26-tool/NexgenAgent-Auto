# Standing video direction

Pasted as-is into "Direct the video" of every video batch (`fill_batch.js` field `direction`). It carries the rules
the shot check kept catching: blends, arms with no body, product drift, light jumps, people walking through the
foreground and impossible mirror reflections (Nov 9 console week). Keep it as one paragraph.

```
Lifestyle footage in the storyboard's look: a real person living with the product in a fully furnished, lived-in room; warm low sunlight with soft long shadows. Each shot takes the size and the camera move its scene row names (wide, medium, close, top-down or low; push-in, dolly, slide, parallax, arc or handheld follow) and moves from its first frame, so no two shots in a row look alike; wide and medium shots keep something in the foreground, the product, and the room behind. Every change of shot is a clean hard cut: no dissolves, crossfades or morphs between shots. The product keeps the exact shape, orientation, proportions, colour and finish of the product photos in every shot, close-ups included: a close-up keeps in frame where the part meets the top and the floor, so its outline stays the photo's; never add or remove parts, doors, drawers or a second piece. Any hand belongs to a person the viewer can place: their shoulder or body is in frame, or the camera is their eyes at standing height and the arm reaches forward from the bottom edge to something at waist or table height. Never hands rising from below to wall height, or coming out of a wall, the product or a window. Every person keeps one body with two arms and two legs in simple poses: standing, walking, or sitting upright with both feet on the floor. ACTOR A is the only person on screen unless the brief names others: nobody walks through the foreground, no leg or arm crosses the frame edge. A person shows in a mirror only when they stand in front of it facing it and the reflection matches them exactly (same clothes, same pose, mirrored); anyone beside or away from a mirror is not in it, and the mirror shows the room. Every shot plays its scene row literally, including the feeling: when a row describes a problem, the people in it look bothered and the problem visibly happens; nobody smiles at a problem. One room, one time of day and one light for the whole video. The product stays planted where it stands, against the same wall or in the same spot in every shot: it never slides, glides, floats, turns by itself or changes size, and it is the same piece with the same shape from the first shot to the last. Props look real and lived-in, never cloned: books have spines of different colours, heights and thicknesses, some stacked flat, never rows of identical blank white books; wine bottles differ in shape, glass colour (green, brown, clear) and label; cups, plates and vases do not repeat as identical copies. No text, logos or captions. Only moves a real camera can make. The furniture is placed and used the way a real person uses it: drawers, doors and lids face the person who uses them and open only when her own hand pulls them; she sits on the side of a desk where the drawers and the knee space are, never behind drawers that face the camera.
```
