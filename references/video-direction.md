# Standing video direction

Pasted as-is into "Direct the video" of every video batch (`fill_batch.js` field `direction`). It carries the rules
the shot check kept catching: blends, arms with no body, product drift, light jumps, people walking through the
foreground and impossible mirror reflections (Nov 9 console week). Keep it as one paragraph.

```
Lifestyle footage in the storyboard's look: a real person living with the product in a fully furnished, lived-in room, mostly eye-level medium and wide shots with something in the foreground, the product, and the room behind; warm low sunlight with soft long shadows. Every change of shot is a clean hard cut: no dissolves, crossfades or morphs between shots. The product keeps the exact shape, orientation, proportions, colour and finish of the product photos in every shot, close-ups included: a close-up keeps in frame where the part meets the top and the floor, so its outline stays the photo's; never add or remove parts, doors, drawers or a second piece. Any hand belongs to a person the viewer can place: their shoulder or body is in frame, or the camera is their eyes at standing height and the arm reaches forward from the bottom edge to something at waist or table height. Never hands rising from below to wall height, or coming out of a wall, the product or a window. Every person keeps one body with two arms and two legs in simple poses: standing, walking, or sitting upright with both feet on the floor. ACTOR A is the only person on screen unless the brief names others: nobody walks through the foreground, no leg or arm crosses the frame edge. A person shows in a mirror only when they stand in front of it facing it and the reflection matches them exactly (same clothes, same pose, mirrored); anyone beside or away from a mirror is not in it, and the mirror shows the room. Every shot plays its scene row literally, including the feeling: when a row describes a problem, the people in it look bothered and the problem visibly happens; nobody smiles at a problem. One room, one time of day and one light for the whole video. No text, logos or captions. Only moves a real camera can make.
```
